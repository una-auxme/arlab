The project focuses on the development of a RoboCup @Home service robot designed to operate safely and effectively in human-centered environments such as households, offices, and public spaces. The robot integrates multiple functional modules that work together to enable perception, decision-making, manipulation, movement, safety, and human interaction. The ultimate goal is to create a robust, modular, and extensible architecture that allows the robot to perform everyday tasks in a safe, reliable, and human-friendly manner.

## Requirements Overview

The functional requirements of the system describe the core capabilities that the robot must provide in order to achieve its objectives within the RoboCup @Home context. They are driven by the need to operate in human-centered environments, perform everyday tasks reliably, and ensure safe interaction with people. These requirements are grouped into six main categories: Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) with Language Model, Decision-Making, Manipulation, Knowledge Base, Computer Vision, Movement with Navigation, and Safety.

They represent the foundation for the robot’s architecture and directly reflect the driving forces of the project:
- Natural and intuitive interaction with humans
- Reliable execution of everyday service tasks
- Robustness in dynamic, real-world environments
- Safety and predictability in human-robot coexistence
- Modularity and extensibility for future development

## Quality Goals

The architecture's top quality goals, prioritized for their importance to project objectives and stakeholder needs:

1. **Safety**  
   Prioritize human safety and system integrity. The robot must operate without causing harm, even under failures or unexpected situations.

2. **Reliability**  
   Ensure that manipulator actions, movement, and database access are consistently successful and dependable.

3. **Robustness**  
   The system must handle diverse objects, environments, and unexpected events without failure.

4. **Real-time Capability**  
   Critical data and safety checks must be performed with minimal delay to ensure responsive and safe operation.

5. **Modularity & Maintainability**  
   Components (manipulator, database, safety mechanisms) should be easily replaceable, maintainable, and upgradable without disrupting the overall system.
